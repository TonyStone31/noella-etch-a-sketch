Faces, front and back

Which side is out, and why it matters.

Every face has a front and a back. The front is the side you are meant to see; the back is drawn in a different color so you can tell at a glance.

When it matters

  • 3D printing. A slicer works out what is inside from which way the faces point. One turned the wrong way and it has to guess.
  • Looking at it. A box with one face inside out reads as a hole that is not there.

For a drawing you are only going to print on paper, it does not matter at all.

Turning one over

  1. Pick the face with the select tool.
  2. Right-click and choose Reverse Face.

Pick several and it turns all of them. The row says how many.

When they come out wrong

Push/pull, revolve and the wizards all get this right on their own - they know which way is out because they built the shape. Faces worked out from loose edges are the ones that can go either way, because a flat loop of lines has no outside until something decides.

/rebuild works the flat areas out again and settles them against their neighbors, which fixes most of it in one go.

Checking a whole model

/holes is the real test: it draws every edge that nothing meets in red. A shape with no red is closed, and a closed shape has a definite inside. See solids and 3D printing.
