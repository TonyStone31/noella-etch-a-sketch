Erase

Delete things.

Keyboard: E

How to use it

  1. Click anything to delete it.
  2. Or hold the button down and drag over several - what the cursor gathers turns red, and none of it goes until you let go.
  3. Or select several and press Delete.
  4. Hold Ctrl and the eraser softens an edge instead of rubbing it out: the edge stops being drawn but the faces either side stay joined. Ctrl+Shift brings a softened edge back.
  5. With Ctrl held the eraser takes only edges, so there is no way to lose a face by aiming badly.

Edges and faces

A face is not a thing sitting there - it is what a closed run of edges encloses. So rubbing out an edge takes any face that was standing on it, the way SketchUp does it: "faces are erased when you erase their bounding edges, opening and reshaping your geometry". Rub one edge off a box and the two sides meeting along it go with it, and you are looking into an open box.

To put one back, draw the line again along where it was. The faces come back with it, and a side traced back into a box rejoins the box rather than sitting loose on top of it. That is SketchUp's healing and it is the only way back to a face you meant to delete and then did not.

Clicking a face away from any edge deletes just that face and leaves every edge standing - an open box with a complete wireframe lid. SketchUp puts that on right-click > Erase rather than on the eraser; here it is on the eraser as well, because that is where you reach for it. Undo brings anything back.
