Orbit

Get about the model.

Keyboard: O - though you will hardly ever want it. Read the next section instead.

The three you actually use

  • Middle-drag spins the model. From any tool, at any point, even half way through drawing a line - which is exactly when you need to see round the back of something. Nothing about what you were doing is lost.
  • Right-drag slides the drawing about.
  • The wheel zooms, towards the pointer, so the thing under the cursor stays under the cursor.

The orbit tool does the same with the left button, for a mouse with no middle button and for a trackpad. Hold Shift while dragging it to pan instead of spin.

Leaving a standard view

Spinning out of PLAN or ISO drops you into the free 3D view first, aimed where you were already looking, so the model does not jump. That is SketchUp's behavior too. V steps back through the standard views - top, front, right and the rest - and I toggles isometric and plan.

Finding your way back

  • Shift+F, or /fit, zooms until it all shows. The one to remember when you are lost.
  • /origin puts the view back on 0,0,0.
  • /cube puts a view cube in the corner: click a face to look square on, an edge for the half turn between two, a corner for the three-quarter view most drawings are read from, or drag it to turn. It also says which way you are facing, which the VIEW button cannot.

Every one of those flies the camera there rather than jumping, and that is on purpose: watching the model turn is what stops you losing your place.
