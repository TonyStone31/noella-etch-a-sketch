Rotate

Turn things about a point.

Keyboard: Q - SketchUp's key for it, not R, which is the rectangle.

How to use it

  1. Pick what you want to turn first. With nothing picked it turns everything joined to what you click, which is usually what you meant but not always.
  2. Click the center it turns about.
  3. Click a point to measure the angle from - anywhere on the thing will do, and a corner is easiest to aim at.
  4. Swing round to the angle and click, or type it and press Enter.

Typing the angle

34.1 is degrees. 8:12 is a slope - eight in twelve, the way a roof pitch is written - and it is there because that is the number on the drawing you are working from, and doing the arctangent in your head to get 33.69 is how mistakes happen.

Which way it turns

The arrow keys pick the plane before you start: left or right stands it upright, up or down lays it flat. The color of the protractor says which - red, green or blue, the same three the axes use. Without that it turns in whatever plane the three points you clicked happen to lie in, which is right most of the time and unhelpful the rest.

Ctrl leaves a copy behind, so the original stays and a turned one appears. After a copy, 3x makes three of them at that angle apart, and /3 divides the turn into three - the way a bolt circle or a row of rafters gets laid out.

What it looks like

Which plane it turns in

The plane comes from the face under the cursor when you click the centre, or from an arrow key - right red, left green, up blue. Alt frees it from the face: it then turns flat unless an arrow says otherwise, and Alt again follows faces again. The protractor works the same way.
