Parts

Making a piece of a drawing a thing of its own. Planned, not built.

This is not in the program yet. The page is here so that the idea has a name and a place before the work starts, and so you know it is coming rather than missing. Other programs call this grouping, and /group will work as another word for it when it arrives.

The problem it solves

Everything you draw joins onto everything else it touches. That is usually what you want - it is why a rectangle drawn against a wall shares that wall's edge, and why a face appears the moment a loop closes.

It is emphatically not what you want when you lay lettering onto the front of a cabinet. The letters land in the same flat plane as the cabinet door, so their edges join the door's edges: the hollow of an R becomes a hole in the door rather than a hole in the R, and rubbing the R out takes a piece of the door with it.

The missing idea is that two pieces of geometry can be in the same place without being the same object. A part is that idea.

What it will do

  • Keep itself to itself. Edges inside a part do not join edges outside it. Nothing merges, nothing gets cut at a crossing, and no face appears spanning the two.
  • Move as one. Click any of it and you have all of it.
  • Lock. A locked part cannot be picked at all, so you can draw against something without any risk of drawing into it - which is the whole reason people reach for this.
  • Open and close. Go inside a part to work on it, and everything else dims and goes out of reach until you come back out.
  • Nest. A drawer is a part; a chest of drawers is a part made of drawers.
  • Explode. Let it go back to being ordinary geometry.

Snapping will still find points inside a closed part, so you can line something up with a corner of it without opening it up first.

Why it is called a part

Because that is what it is - a part of the thing you are drawing. Most programs say "group", and that word will work too, because it is what people type. The reason for not simply copying SketchUp here is what sits underneath the word: they have two of these, a Group and a Component, and which one you should have used is the single most confusing thing in the program. One idea seems better than two, so a part will know how many copies of itself exist, and a copy is either linked to the original or cut loose from it. One thing to learn instead of two.

When

After the drawing tools are solid. Parts sit on top of picking, moving, erasing, snapping and the way faces are worked out from edges - so building them first would mean fixing every fault underneath them twice, and hiding some of those faults until much later. The plan is written up in full in docs/groupplan.md in the source.

In the meantime, the way to keep lettering off a face is to draw it on its own sheet, or to lay it just clear of the face rather than exactly on it.
