Typing measurements

You can type instead of dragging, nearly everywhere.

  • Feet and inches: 12', 8", 12'6", 12' 6 1/2".
  • Plain numbers are in whatever units the drawing is in.
  • A rectangle takes 12'x8'.
  • An angle takes 34.1, or 8:12 for a slope.
  • Truss notation. 6-8-15 is six foot eight and fifteen sixteenths. Feet, then inches, then sixteenths - the way a truss drawing writes a length, and the way the component design software the shops run prints it. Every field is a whole number, there is no foot mark, inch mark or slash anywhere in it, and the whole thing goes in from the number pad with the minus key. 0-8-8 is eight and a half inches; that leading nought for the feet is how a truss sheet writes anything under a foot.
  • The sixteenths are whatever the drawing is counting in - the ROUNDED TO setting along the bottom. A last field at or above that is refused rather than guessed at, because being quietly wrong by a hair on a length somebody cuts metal from is worse than being told.
  • It works anywhere a length is wanted: a line, a side of a rectangle, a radius, a push, a move, the fitting wizards, retyping a dimension. For a rectangle it still wants both sides - 6-8-15x4-0-0, or 6-8-15/4-0-0 from the number pad.
  • 12-6, with one dash, is twelve foot six - the older, shorter form of the same idea.
  • With the circle or arc tool live, a number with an s on it - 24s - sets how many sides or segments it is drawn with. + and - do the same one at a time.
  • A slash starts a command instead of a measurement - see commands.
